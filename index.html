About

I am an undergraduate student in the Department of Automation at Tsinghua University. My research uses controlled experiments to study the behavior of language-model agents and reasoning systems.

A recurring theme in my work is the gap between what a model appears to know and what actually governs its later behavior. I am interested in action allocation, revision, procedural control, behavioral internalization, and evaluation methods that preserve these distinctions instead of compressing them into a single endpoint.

Research

Research interests

AI agents & model behavior

How tool-using and coding agents allocate actions, react to evidence, construct procedural policies, and change behavior across a trajectory.

Reasoning, revision & internalization

When a model's own judgement becomes causally binding for later behavior, and when explicit recognition, reflection, or successful training leaves the operative policy largely unchanged.

Evaluation & cognitive architectures

Experimental designs that separate endpoint performance from process behavior, and architectures in which evaluation, control, memory, and adaptation become native learned capabilities rather than external scaffolding alone.

More broadly, I am interested in the boundary between machine learning, cognitive science, and interactive intelligent systems.
